Adidas Tokyo

A neo-noir concept film told from the point of view of the person being walked toward. A hired assassin called Tokyo arrives, the tension builds, the shot is fired — and the last thing the victim sees is the sneaker “Tokyo”.

The premise

The last shoe you’ll see.

A shoe campaign normally opens on the shoe. Here the sneaker is written into the brief as critical and not to be revealed explicitly until the final scene, so every beat before it is spent building the tension the product has to occupy. The most memorable frame in the film is the one it spends the whole film refusing to show.

Structure

Build-up, then payoff.

Nine boarded beats on one rising line, every one of them shot through a lens or from below, so the audience never gets to be the one doing the approaching.

  1. Scene I

    The elevator

    CCTV, top-down, cold. Tokyo stands still, then looks up into the lens: “My name is Tokyo.”

  2. Scene II

    The door

    A corridor at a Dutch angle, a doorbell pressed into a fisheye lens, the signal tearing. “You ask why?”

  3. Scene III

    The floor

    Fragments — steps, aiming, the fall. “Because…”, delivered with pauses. Then ground level, a figure standing over, and the shoe.

How it was built

Pipeline.

The character was locked before the film was, because a generative shoot drifts. A face, a tracksuit, and a sneaker all had to survive nine scenes without quietly redesigning themselves.

Reference

Pinterest crossed with Midjourney portraits until one face held: fine features, uneven shoulder-length hair, a bandage on the cheek.

Contract

The result written down as keywords and marked critical — the guarded eyes, the navy tracksuit, the sneaker's exact proportions. None of them free to change.

Generate

A character sheet built first, then every scene and product frame generated from it, with Higgsfield carrying the motion.

Release

Cut vertical and posted as numbered stripes rather than one film, so the payoff arrives on its own.
